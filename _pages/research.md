My work explores how the spatial distribution of urban resources shapes unequal access to opportunities. I primarily use GIS, walking-network accessibility modelling, spatial statistics and planning simulations.

## 15-Minute Education Circles and Spatial Equity

**Study area:** Beijing, China  
**Methods:** GIS; pedestrian-network accessibility; population-weighted assessment; spatial regression; fixed-supply redistribution scenarios.

I developed a multi-stage accessibility framework covering kindergartens, primary schools, and secondary schools within a 15-minute walking threshold. The analysis identified substantial core–periphery differences. A fixed-supply ideal-city benchmark suggested a potential **55.2% reduction in service-pressure inequality** through improved spatial matching between school capacity and demand.

**Published output:** Zhu, K., Zhu, S., & Yan, X. (2026), *Cities*, Article 107506. [Read the paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cities.2026.107506).

## Urban Green-Space Accessibility and Housing Prices

**Study area:** Shanghai, China  
**Methods:** GIS; pedestrian network analysis; OLS and spatial econometric modelling.

This research experience examines the association between network-based access to urban green space and residential property prices. The work contributes analytical experience in accessibility measurement and spatial dependence.

## Lifelong Learning Accessibility for Older Adults

**Study scope:** Four Chinese cities  
**Focus:** Access to lifelong learning facilities for older residents.

Participated in research on the availability and accessibility of lifelong learning opportunities for older adults, supported by academic supervision.
