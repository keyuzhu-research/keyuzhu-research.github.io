## About Me

Hello! I am **Keyu Zhu**, a master's student at the School of Design, East China Normal University, Shanghai, China. I expect to graduate in June 2027.

My research focuses on **urban accessibility, spatial equity, and the distribution of public services**. I am particularly interested in understanding how differences in the spatial distribution of urban resources shape people's access to opportunities.

I use Geographic Information Systems (GIS), pedestrian network analysis, spatial statistics, and spatial optimization to investigate urban accessibility and evaluate potential planning interventions.

## Research Interests

- **15-Minute Cities** — Urban accessibility and proximity-based planning.
- **Spatial Equity** — Unequal access to educational facilities and other public services.
- **GIS and Spatial Analysis** — Network-based accessibility modelling and spatial statistics.
- **Urban Planning and Optimization** — Facility allocation and spatial redistribution.
- **Urban Opportunities** — Relationships between urban resources, residential locations, and access to opportunities.

## Research Experience

### 15-Minute Education Circles and Spatial Equity

Developed a GIS-based framework to evaluate walking accessibility to educational facilities across different educational stages. The research also examines how facility reallocation can improve spatial coverage and equity.

### Urban Green Space Accessibility and Housing Prices

Investigated relationships between urban green space accessibility and residential property prices in Shanghai, using network analysis and spatial econometric methods.

### Accessibility to Lifelong Learning Facilities

Participated in research examining access to lifelong learning facilities for older adults across four Chinese cities.

## Academic Interests

My broader academic interest is guided by a central question:

**Why do people living in the same city have different opportunities to access urban resources?**

I hope to further investigate the relationships between urban planning, residential opportunities, and spatial inequality.
